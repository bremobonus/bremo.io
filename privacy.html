Privacy Notice

Last updated: 16 July 2026. This is a plain-language summary and a starting template. Before Bremo collects real personal or health information from the public, this notice must be reviewed by a qualified privacy lawyer and finalised for the jurisdictions we operate in.

Health information is sensitive. We treat what you share in an intake as confidential and collect only what we need to match you well.

What we collect

  • Intake details you choose to share — the kind of support you want, your situation, preferences, location, language, and contact details.
  • Provider details from professionals applying to the network — name, contact, profession, regulator and registration number, specialties and availability.
  • Basic technical data needed to run the site securely.

How we use it

  • To understand your needs and match you with an appropriate, verified professional.
  • To verify that professionals are licensed and in good standing.
  • To contact you about your match or application.
  • To keep the service safe, including routing apparent emergencies to crisis resources.

What we don't do

  • We don't sell your personal information.
  • We don't share your intake beyond what's needed to arrange your care, and only with your knowledge.
  • We don't use your health information for advertising.

Your choices & rights

You can ask us what we hold about you, correct it, or ask us to delete it. Under Canadian privacy law (such as PIPEDA and, for health information, provincial laws like Ontario's PHIPA), you have rights over your personal information. To make a request, contact us at the address below.

How we protect it

We limit collection to what's necessary, restrict access, and use reasonable safeguards. No system is perfectly secure, and this notice will be updated as our safeguards and infrastructure are finalised.

Contact

Questions or requests: privacy@bremo.io.

This template does not constitute legal advice. Bremo Care is early-stage; sections marked for review must be completed with qualified legal counsel before handling real personal or health data.
